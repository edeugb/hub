## The structural part (memorize this first)

What makes everything different from B2C isn't a matter of degree — it's structural:

- **Buyer ≠ user ≠ champion.** The one who signs (economic buyer), the one who uses (end user), and the one who evaluates/pushes internally (champion) are different people with different motivations. Every product decision must answer to all three.
- **Buying committees of 6–10 people** and **sales cycles of 3–12 months** (≈84 days for deals <$25K ACV; 6+ months in enterprise). What I do today bills in months.
- **Contracts** — clauses, compliance, SLAs. Business viability (Cagan) weighs more than in B2C.
- **Expansion/NRR as the growth engine.** Per the High Alpha 2025 SaaS Benchmarks Report, companies above $50M ARR generate ~60% of their new ARR from existing customers, not new logos.

## Unit economics — the minimum vocabulary

| Metric | What it is | Healthy reference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| MRR / ARR | Monthly / annual recurring revenue (exclude one-off fees and services) | — |
| NRR | (Starting MRR + expansion − contraction − churn) / starting MRR | >100% = you grow without selling to anyone new; median ~101–106% |
| GRR | Retention without expansion (capped at 100%) | "It keeps NRR honest" |
| CAC | Cost of acquiring a customer | 5–10× more expensive to acquire than to retain |
| CAC Payback | Months to recover CAC | <12–18 healthy; current median ~16–20 |
| LTV:CAC | Lifetime value vs acquisition cost | ~3:1 floor; >5:1 may signal under-investment in growth |
| Gross margin | — | Retaining runs at 80%+ margins |
| Magic Number | New ARR per S&M dollar | 1.0+; top quartile >2.0 |
| Rule of 40 | Growth % + margin % ≥ 40 | Only ~16–34% of SaaS companies hit it depending on the year |

**Why retention beats acquisition** (the thread connecting everything):

1. Acquiring costs 5–10× more than retaining → if users don't stay, expensive acquisition replaces lost revenue forever.
2. CAC is a "loan" recovered month by month → churn before payback = a direct cash-flow loss on that account.
3. LTV is multiplied by lifespan → lowering churn makes LTV skyrocket, and selling expansion to a trusting customer is far cheaper than selling to a stranger.
4. Retention has very high margins (development and onboarding are already paid for) and pushes the Magic Number up.

## Positioning, GTM motion, and ACV must align

| ACV | Motion | Characteristics |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Low (<$5K) | Pure PLG | User = buyer; self-serve; you need ~10× more customers |
| Mid ($5K–$50K) | Hybrid | Self-serve to land, sales to expand. **Today's default above ~$10M ARR** |
| High (>$50K) | Sales-led | Buying committee, demo → pilot → contract, long cycles |

:::tip[Operating rule]
Before opining on roadmap, pricing, or channel at any company: locate its ACV, its current motion, and its NRR. Those three coordinates tell you which game is being played.
:::

## Practice

Take a company you know (or a consulting client) and fill in the table: ACV, motion, NRR, CAC payback. Are motion and ACV aligned? Does the declared strategy match the coordinates?
