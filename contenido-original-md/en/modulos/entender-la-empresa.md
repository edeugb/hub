This module is my **day 1–30 checklist** when joining a new company or starting a consulting engagement.

## 2.1 Business model

- **Revenue streams**: subscription, usage, setup, services. Which are recurring and which are not?
- **Pricing and packaging**: per seat, per usage, per module, per tier.

> 📌 *Case to document: how this works at GeoVictoria (→ Applied cases).*

## 2.2 The queen metrics

Ask for **total ARR and MRR** and per module, and the **NRR** (or the leakage %):

- NRR = expansion − contraction − churn over the base. **>100% = you grow without selling to anyone new.** Reference: >101% (ChartMogul, Benchmarkit).
- Acquisition brings in new sales, but **retention defends and guarantees the baseline**: with high retention, the ARR base grows predictably every month and lowers the pressure to start from zero every quarter.
- These metrics outrank CAC, CAC Payback, and LTV in the hierarchy (see [Fundamentals](/en/modulos/fundamentos/)).
- Also ask: **ARPA / ACV** (your real "customer size") and **average sales cycle** (~80 days typical in B2B).

### Quick diagnosis when the numbers are bad

| Symptom | Where to look | Product-side response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **High churn** | CAC Payback (churn before payback = direct cash loss) | Why do they leave? Product, processes, CX, CS. Attack frictions; quality from launch (no falling into miscalculations or malfunctions) |
| **Low MRR** | The pains we solve | We're not solving profitable pains → back to problem discovery |

## 2.3 The B2B asymmetry

Understand **who buys** (economic buyer/decision-maker) ≠ **who uses** (end user) ≠ **who evaluates** (champion). In B2B, "the user and the buyer are typically very different people, with very different concerns, motivations, and needs" — and value and viability risk dominates.

Also ask: sales-led or product-led? What's the sales cycle?

## 2.4 Strategy and the three levers

What's the current strategy? Raise the average ticket? Compete on price? Specialization or service quality?

**Every B2B SaaS feature ends up moving one of these three levers:**

| Lever | What it is | What it looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A · Acquisition** | Win new customers | Open markets, lower buying friction, close gaps that lose deals |
| **B · Expansion** | Current customers pay more | Upsell, cross-sell, new modules. Raises NRR |
| **C · Retention** | They don't leave | Lower churn by attacking the pain that makes a customer consider leaving |

:::tip[Key question for your boss]
"Of these three levers, which one was I hired to move this year?" — All your discovery organizes itself around that answer.
:::

## 2.5 Completing the context

- Most relevant or focus industries, and why.
- Current OKRs or goals.
- Expected business outcomes and the company's most relevant metrics (e.g., at GeoVictoria, sales / MRR).
- Methodologies the team already uses — **understand before proposing** (Principle 01).

## Practice

Run this full checklist against your current company and write the answers down. The gaps you can't answer are your week-1 conversation agenda.

## Artifact

- A 2-page "Company brief": model, queen metrics with numbers, assigned lever, strategy, initial stakeholder map.
