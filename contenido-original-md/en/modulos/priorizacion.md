## 7.1 The central trade-off for a B2B PM

**Deal-driven roadmap (sales-led chaos) vs strategy-driven roadmap.**

The mature answer: split capacity into explicit percentages and defend them —

- A **protected % for strategic bets**
- A **% for commercial commitments**
- A **% for technical debt / quality**

(This connects with [Initiative × metric](/en/modulos/entender-el-producto/#33-initiative-type--metric-it-moves): technical debt doesn't compete with features on NRR — it competes for its protected percentage.)

## 7.2 ICP as a filter

Customer commitments (big deals, contract clauses, roadmap promised in sales) distort pure prioritization. Defense:

- **A feature wanted by 80% of users outside the ICP but only 5% inside the ICP is not a priority.**
- At scale, weight by impact on ICP-heavy cohorts (Gartner: ~80% of future B2B revenue comes from ~20% of customers, which usually coincide with the ICP).

## 7.3 Scoring frameworks — and their limits

*(Complement)* Standard frameworks serve as a shared language, not an oracle:

- **RICE** (Reach × Impact × Confidence / Effort): useful for comparing heterogeneous initiatives; the danger is the false precision of made-up numbers.
- **WSJF** (Cost of Delay / Duration): useful when the cost of waiting is real (regulatory deadlines — very relevant in compliance, where a fine has a date).
- **Value vs Effort (2×2)**: enough for executive conversations.

**What's valuable isn't the score: it's documenting the decisions where the score said one thing and you decided another, with the justification.** That's where judgment lives.

## Practice

Take a real prioritization cycle I've run: document the inputs (expected MRR, risk, commercial blockage, strategic fit), the scoring, and — most valuable — **2 decisions where the score said one thing and I decided another**, with the justification.
