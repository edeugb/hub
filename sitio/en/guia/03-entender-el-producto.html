3 · Understanding the product

Main product, additional modules, current metrics for each.

3.1 The base questions per product

  • What job does it solve, and for whom? (JTBD — see Discovery)
  • What stage is it in? Exploration (seeking fit), growth (scaling), maturity (defending).
  • The stage decides the metric: fit and activation vs. expansion vs. retention and efficiency.
  • Walk the happy path and above all the exceptions — that's where a PM's value lives.

3.2 The lifecycle decides the playbook

The type and amount of discovery, how demand is generated, and how it's validated change radically by stage. Classic model (Levitt, «Exploit the Product Life Cycle», HBR 1965; Vernon 1966): Development → Introduction → Growth → Maturity → Decline.

Operating rule: don't apply the same playbook at different stages. Early stages play offense (generative discovery, value risk); maturity plays defense (retention, efficiency, NRR).

3.3 Initiative type × metric it moves

(Answer to my open question: yes, there's a relationship, and it pays to make it explicit before prioritizing.)

Each initiative type has a «natural» primary metric and metrics where its effect is indirect or nil. Evaluating an initiative with the wrong metric kills it unfairly (or approves it without reason).

InitiativePrimary metricEffect on NRREffect on CAC / paybackHow to justify it
New feature (expansion)Module attach rate, expansion ARR⬆ Direct (expansion raises NRR)Neutral-positive: selling to the installed base has ~0 CAC, improves blended paybackLever B: «this module adds $X of expansion MRR to Y% of the base»
New feature (acquisition)Win rate, pipeline generated, unblocked dealsNeutral short-term⬇ CAC if win rate rises (same S&M spend, more closes)Lever A: «we close the gap losing us Z deals/quarter»
Technical debtNone direct — it's an enabler⬆ Indirect: fewer incidents → less quality-driven churn⬆ Indirect: velocity → more A/B/C initiatives per quarterTranslate it into risk: «without this, each release costs X% more and enterprise incident risk is Y». Never sell it as «cleanup»
Retention fixChurn / GRR, champion's weekly usage (leading)⬆ Direct (less contraction/churn)⬆ Payback: every extra month of customer life amortizes the CAC already paidLever C: «accounts with this pain churn at X%, the fix protects $Y of MRR»

Rule: technical debt and infrastructure don't compete with features on the same metric — they compete for a protected % of capacity (see Prioritization). Trying to justify them with direct NRR is losing the argument.
