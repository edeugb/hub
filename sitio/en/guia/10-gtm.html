10 · Go-to-Market

10.1 GTM ≠ launch

GTM = target segment + positioning + pricing/packaging + channel + sales motion + enablement + launch plan. The launch is the last 10%.

Launch tiers

Not every launch deserves the same. Defining the tier BEFOREHAND saves fighting with marketing later.

TierTreatment
1Event, PR, campaign
2Email to the base, webinar, strong enablement
3Release notes, docs

10.2 Positioning — April Dunford, Obviously Awesome (2019)

In B2B, segment by firmographics first. Three strategies:

  1. Head-to-head — win an existing market.
  2. Big fish, small pond — dominate a subsegment.
  3. Create a new game — a new category.

Also: Sales Pitch (Dunford) and Crossing the Chasm (Moore) for adoption by segments.

10.3 ICP (Ideal Customer Profile)

A well-defined ICP says «no» to most — «mid-market SaaS with 100–500 employees in North America» is not an ICP: it describes ~40,000 companies.

  • Commercial validation: higher win rate, shorter cycle, better retention/expansion in ICP vs non-ICP accounts.
  • Audit annually.

10.4 GTM motions and GTM fit

Motion ↔ ACV relationship: pure PLG <$5K; hybrid $5K–$50K; sales-led >$50K with 3–12-month cycles and a buying committee (~84 days for deals <$25K; 6+ months enterprise; 6–10 stakeholders).

What «GTM fit» is and why to sequence

(Answer to my open question — developed.)

GTM fit is the milestone that comes AFTER product-market fit and BEFORE scaling. PMF says «someone wants this»; GTM fit says «I have a repeatable and profitable way of reaching those who want it»:

  • Concrete evidence: the same motion (e.g., outbound to HR at Chilean retail) generates pipeline predictably, with acceptable CAC payback and win rate, without depending on the founders or luck.
  • Without GTM fit, scaling multiplies a process that doesn't work: you hire 5 salespeople for a playbook that doesn't exist.

Sequence, don't parallelize means: dominate ONE segment (one ICP, one motion, one message) before adding another.

  • Why: every new segment demands its own message, pricing, channel, and enablement. Splitting focus between two half-validated segments doubles the cost and validates neither.
  • The cited benchmark (~7–10 months to validate a motion at a B2B company with early traction) reflects the B2B sales cycle: you need to see complete deals — from first contact to close and first renewals — to know whether the motion works. With 3–6-month cycles, you can't judge a motion in one quarter.
  • The signal to expand to the next segment: the current one generates predictable pipeline with positive unit economics and the playbook is documented (someone new can run it).

10.5 Pricing & packaging

  • Value metric: do you charge per seat, per managed employee, per transaction? It's the most important pricing decision.
  • Good/Better/Best and feature gating as an expansion lever.
  • Van Westendorp and WTP interviews to calibrate.
  • Book: Monetizing Innovation (Ramanujam) — pricing is defined during discovery, not after building.

10.6 Sales enablement as a product

Battle cards, demo scripts, one-pagers, ROI calculators, objection FAQs. In sales-led B2B, enablement determines whether the feature sells or dies in the CRM.

10.7 Positioning by segment/country

Same feature, different pain per market (e.g., compliance weighs differently in Chile vs Mexico). Multi-country GTM demands localized messages, not translated ones.

Practice

Build the complete GTM for a real launch: segment, message per buyer persona (HR vs Finance vs IT), tier, enablement kit, commercial success metric (pipeline generated, attach rate, time to first sale).

Artifact

  • A 1-page GTM plan (my own template).
  • A battle card + one-pager for a real feature (anonymized).
  • A note: «How the same product's message changes between 2 countries».
