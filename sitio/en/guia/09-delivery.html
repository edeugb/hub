9 · Delivery, team, and stakeholders

📝 Note

This was the briefest chapter in the original document — it's been complemented (sections 9.3 and 9.4) because in senior interviews and consulting, stakeholder management weighs as much as discovery.

9.1 Landing questions

  • How is prioritization done today, how is the backlog managed, and how do bugs come in?
  • Technical debt: ask about it explicitly — it conditions what can be promised.
  • Stakeholders, and who holds the «casting vote» on each topic.
  • If the product team sits inside IT: they'll push for the product to be technically perfect; I'll push for commercial traction. Make that tension explicit from day 1.

9.2 Technical debt as a business conversation

(Complement — connects with Initiative × metric.)

  • Never sell it as «cleanup»: translate it into risk (incident probability × affected accounts × MRR at stake) or velocity (which A/B/C lever initiatives get unblocked).
  • Negotiate a fixed % of capacity per cycle instead of fighting item by item.

9.3 Roadmap (complement)

  • Now / Next / Later instead of dates per quarter: it communicates intent without committing to dates that sales will turn into contract clauses.
  • Roadmap by outcome («reduce mid-market churn») instead of by feature, when the audience allows it.
  • Two views of the same roadmap: internal (outcomes, bets, honest uncertainty) and commercial (themes and broad windows). Never hand the internal one to a customer.

9.4 Stakeholders (complement)

  • Power × interest map: manage closely the high-power + high-interest; keep informed the high-power + low-interest; don't over-invest in the rest.
  • Sales is the structural stakeholder in B2B: fixed rituals (a monthly review of deals blocked by product) prevent escalation via WhatsApp.
  • Decisions in writing: a short decision record (context → options → decision → who decided → date). Cheap to maintain, saves arguments six months later.
  • Disagreement: explicit disagree and commit over false consensus.

Practice

Draw the power × interest map of my current context and check where I'm over- or under-investing time.

Artifact

  • A product ↔ commercial ↔ operations communication protocol (I already built one at GeoVictoria → document it as a case).
  • A decision-record template.
